La Ceibona weather station · IESPAR102 · Mojón de Esparza, Costa Rica

Our light sensor has read about 1.35× too high since 25 August 2026

We compared our solar radiation and UV readings with two nearby stations, minute by minute, for June to September of 2025 and 2026. Both neighbours carried on as before; ours stepped up on about 25 August and stayed there. The same dates in 2025 show no change, so it is our sensor, not the season or the sky.

    Our daily peak jumped; the neighbours' did not

    Each day's highest solar reading traces the sensor's ceiling, since most days have at least a few minutes of clear sky. Ours held near 1,050 W/m² through June to August, as in 2025, then jumped to about 1,425. IESPAR72 and IPUNTA186 barely moved.

    At the same moment, we now read about a third more than both neighbours

    This is the sharpest test. For every 5 minutes between 10:00 and 13:00 when both stations read above 300 W/m², we divide our reading by the neighbour's, then take each day's median. The different sensors and sites mean the ratio was never exactly 1; what matters is whether it moves.

    Lines: 7-day rolling median. Dots: single days (clouds over one station but not the other make single days noisy). The grey line is the same ratio against IESPAR72 on the same dates of 2025.

    The rise from 0.89 in 2025 to 1.01 in early 2026 is on IESPAR72's side: its own daily peaks fell from about 1,170 to 1,040 W/m² while ours stayed near 1,050.

    The same increase at every light level and most hours

    A reflection from something nearby would add light only at certain sun angles, and dirt on the sensor would lower the readings. Instead the jump is about ×1.3–1.4 whether the sky is dim or bright, and from 8:00 to 14:00. That pattern points to a change in the sensor's sensitivity.

    UV shows the same jump

    The Tempest works out UV from the same light sensor, so it should move in step, and it does.

    What to check on the station

    • The white diffuser dome on top of the Tempest: cracks, scratches, discoloration, or anything else that changed its look.
    • The firmware version in the Tempest app, and whether it updated around 25 August (the station reported fw 179 in September).
    • If nothing is visibly wrong, send these findings to WeatherFlow support: a step of about 1.35× in both solar and UV from about 25 August 2026, at all light levels, not seen at a nearby Tempest (IESPAR72).

    Until it is fixed, the dashboard marks UV and solar figures after 25 August as under review. ETo and the water balance use solar radiation too, so they are somewhat overstated for the same weeks.

    Limits of this comparison

    • The neighbours use other sensors at other sites, so only changes in the ratios carry meaning, not the absolute values.
    • IPUNTA186 only reports from 30 July 2026: 24 days before the change and none in 2025.
    • A few earlier days (16, 18 and 20 August) already show the high ratio, so the change may have come and gone for about a week before it stuck.
    • Local clouds make single days noisy; the medians over weeks are what count.
